## The task

A2 is a portfolio in three parts. Part A credits the practical work you
have been doing in the asynchronous sessions. Part B checks, under quiz
conditions, that the ideas behind that work are yours. Part C is the
main event: take an exploratory artefact, define who it is for, and
build the explanatory pivot as a public dashboard.

### Part A · Asynchronous sessions (15 marks)

Each asynchronous session on the course site ends in artefacts you
create yourself and submit through Canvas. Session 1's five are already
specified on the site:

1. the final chart of your amniote investigation, with panels at
   different levels of aggregation;
2. your tree-of-life figure;
3. your Triangle of Everything chart;
4. the alternative periodic-table design you found (not the table you
   built);
5. your codon chart.

Sessions 2 and 3 add their own deliverables when released
[confirm counts]. Fifteen deliverables across the three sessions, one
mark each.

A deliverable earns its mark when it is submitted on time, is your own
work, and meets the requirements stated on its session page (right
panels, right labels, a readable finished chart, not a screenshot of
half-finished code). There are no part marks within a deliverable.

### Part B · Mid-semester quiz (25 marks)

One quiz, 25 questions, one mark each, auto-marked on Canvas. Closed
book, no AI assistance. [Confirm: sat in the live session under
supervision, or a timed at-home window.]

The quiz covers the first half of the course: the critique fields and
claim taxonomy from Seeing Data; encodings, tidy data and the Visual
Vocabulary from Choosing Visual Forms; and the reasoning threaded
through the asynchronous sessions (levels of aggregation, log scales
and ratios, reading trees and clades, what a layout choice shows and
hides). If you did the asynchronous work yourself, the quiz holds no
surprises; that is its point.

One attempt. 40 minutes. Questions are drawn per-student from pools, so
papers differ.

### Part C · Dashboards (60 marks)

Take an exploratory artefact and pivot it into an explanatory one for a
person you can name.

1. **Choose an exploratory starting point.** A Tableau Public
   Visualisation of the Day, or a dashboard built on an approved
   dataset [confirm the approved list]. An exploratory artefact lets a
   reader wander; your job is to decide where a specific reader needs
   to go.
2. **Define the persona.** A named, plausible user with a role, a
   context, a decision they face and the constraints they work under.
   Not "the general public".
3. **Write the user stories.** At least three, in the standard form:
   as [the persona], I need to [task] so that [decision or outcome].
   Each story names a concrete visual task (compare, rank, locate,
   track change over time) that your dashboard must support.
4. **Prepare the data.** Document the source, the licence, every
   transformation, and what the prepared extract can and cannot
   support. The data-handling standards from A1 carry over unchanged.
5. **Build the explanatory pivot** as a Tableau Public dashboard. Every
   view on it must serve a user story; anything decorative or
   unexplained counts against it. Layout, titles, annotations and
   interactivity are design decisions and will be judged as such.
6. **Write the justification report** (at most 1,500 words): the
   persona defence, the user stories, a walk from each story to the
   view that serves it, the Visual Vocabulary category of each view
   with the genuine alternative you rejected and why, and the
   limitations of data and design. Include the standard AI-use
   declaration.

**Submit:** the public dashboard URL; the packaged workbook (`.twbx`);
the prepared data with its documentation; and the justification report
as PDF, in one ZIP.

**AI status.** Allowed with declaration for Part C, consistent with A1
Part C conventions: any AI assistance in analysis, design or writing is
disclosed in the declaration. Part B is AI-free by its conditions.
Part A follows the asynchronous sessions' own policy.

### Part C · Dashboards (60 marks, human-marked, anchored)

Each item is marked on its anchors; anchor descriptors below the table.

| ID | Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|---:|
| C1 | Persona and user stories: specific, plausible, decision-focused; each story names a visual task | 10 |
| C2 | Data preparation and provenance: source, licence, transformations documented; extract honestly scoped | 10 |
| C3 | Dashboard craft: encoding choices, layout, titles, annotation, interactivity; every view serves a story | 20 |
| C4 | The pivot itself: the explanatory reshaping demonstrably serves the persona better than the exploratory original; before/after reasoning is explicit | 10 |
| C5 | Justification report: story-to-view walk, Vocabulary categories with rejected alternatives, limitations, AI declaration | 10 |

**Anchors (applied per criterion).** Top band: the criterion is met
with evidence, and the choices are defended against genuine
alternatives. Middle band: met in substance with gaps in evidence or
defence. Low band: asserted rather than demonstrated. Zero: absent or
generic enough to fit any dashboard.

**Marking notes.**
- C3 is the largest criterion and is marked view-by-view: a view that
  serves no user story caps C3 at half marks.
- A dashboard whose public link is broken at marking time is marked
  from the `.twbx` with a 5-mark deduction against C3 [confirm].
- The persona defence is individual work; the A3 group phase begins
  after A2 and builds on it.
